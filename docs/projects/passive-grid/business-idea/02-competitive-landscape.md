## Executive summary

The atmospheric water generation (AWG) market splits into three mechanism families: **refrigeration/condensation** (dominant in consumer), **sorbent + heat regeneration** (SOURCE, Spout, AirJoule, Atoco), and **passive solar still / desiccant cycles** (mostly research, no scaled consumer SKU). Refrigeration AWGs (Watergen, Skywell, Tsunami, Akvo) own the plug-in residential segment but require **≥20–30% RH**, high electricity, and grid power. SOURCE Hydropanels occupy the **off-grid sorbent** niche with proven low-RH operation (down to ~10% RH) but at **$5,500+ installed**, large 4×8 ft footprint, and low yield (2–5 L/panel/day).

**Passive-grid's white space:** a **portable, zero-power, expeditionary sorbent harvester** with replaceable cartridges — a form factor no incumbent sells today. MOF commercialization (AirJoule, Atoco) is a medium-term threat at industrial scale, not in the sub-$500 preparedness segment passive-grid targets first.

## Market landscape overview

### Mechanism families

| Family | How it works | Typical RH floor | Energy model | Maturity |
|--------|--------------|------------------|--------------|----------|
| **Refrigeration AWG** | Cool air below dew point; condense vapor | ~20–30% RH (rated); practical output drops sharply below ~35% | Grid AC, 250–400 Wh/L | Commercial — dozens of SKUs |
| **Desiccant wheel + powered regen** | Hygroscopic wheel adsorbs; electric heater desorbs | ~20% RH (Spout); continuous cycle | Grid AC, ~380–600 W | Emerging consumer (Spout) |
| **Solar sorbent panel** | Hygroscopic material + solar thermal desorption | ~10% RH (SOURCE) | Fully off-grid solar | Commercial — SOURCE only at scale |
| **MOF sorbent (powered)** | MOF-coated contactors; vacuum + low-grade heat | ~20–30% RH (AirJoule) | Grid or waste heat | Pre-commercial / industrial |
| **Passive sorbent + solar still** | Night adsorption, day solar desorption/condensation | Lab-validated to ~15% RH with MOFs; zeolite higher | **Zero external power** | Research / prototype only |

### Market structure

```
                    HIGH ENERGY / GRID-DEPENDENT
                              │
    Tsunami T-500 ────────────┼──────── Watergen GENNY+
    Aquaria Hydropack ────────┼──────── Skywell 5
    Akvo Pod ─────────────────┼──────── Spout Monolith
                              │
    ──────────────────────────┼────────────────────────── LOW RH CAPABILITY
                              │
    AirJoule Core/Prime ──────┼──────── Atoco (container)
                              │
    SOURCE Hydropanels ───────┤
                              │
                    PASSIVE / OFF-GRID
                              │
                    ★ passive-grid target ★
                    (portable, zero-power, cartridge)
```

Refrigeration incumbents cluster in the **high-energy, moderate-RH** quadrant. SOURCE and MOF players push toward **lower RH** but remain **large-format or powered**. No commercial product occupies **passive + portable + cartridge-replaceable** today.

## Competitor profiles

### 1. SOURCE Global (Hydropanels)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Mechanism** | Hygroscopic desiccant + solar thermal desorption; fully off-grid |
| **Price** | ~$2,950/panel deposit; **$4,500–$6,500** for typical 2-panel residential install ([SOURCE FAQ](https://source.co/pages/frequently-asked-questions), [EnergyBS 2026 guide](https://energybs.com/green-living/water/atmospheric-water-generator-awg-hydropanel-guide/)) |
| **RH range** | **10–100%** RH rated ([World Water Reserve review](https://worldwaterreserve.com/source-hydropanels/)) |
| **Output** | 2–5 L/panel/day (0.5–1.3 gal) |
| **Energy** | Zero grid electricity; integrated solar PV + thermal |
| **Target customer** | Off-grid homeowners, drought regions, disaster resilience, corporate ESG |
| **Strengths** | Proven low-RH sorbent path; 15-year service life; no plumbing; strong brand |
| **Weaknesses** | Large footprint (4×8 ft, 340 lb/panel); low daily yield; high installed cost; roof/ground mount required; not portable |

**Relevance to passive-grid:** Closest mechanism analog (sorbent + solar thermal) but opposite form factor — SOURCE is fixed infrastructure; passive-grid is expeditionary/portable.

## White-space and positioning gaps

### Gaps passive-grid can own

1. **Zero-power expeditionary form factor** — No competitor sells a cylinder/MOLLE-mount passive harvester. SOURCE requires roof/ground arrays; Spout requires outlet.

2. **Preparedness / grid-down segment** — Refrigeration AWGs become useless in extended blackout. SOURCE works but is immovable and expensive. passive-grid's night/day cycle needs no electronics.

3. **Sub-$500 entry price** — Prototype BOM $561–638 (powered bench) vs passive kit target **<$200** ([`00-passive-grid-down-spec.md`](00-passive-grid-down-spec.md)). Undercuts every commercial AWG by 5–30×.

4. **Replaceable sorbent cartridge (razor/blade)** — Recurring revenue from desiccant pucks; no incumbent uses this model in consumer AWG.

5. **Honest yield positioning** — Competitors over-market rated output. passive-grid can win trust by claiming **supplemental/emergency** liters, not primary household supply.

6. **Humid-variable climates (Ontario)** — Many competitors optimize for desert/sunbelt. Ontario's seasonal RH swings suit **night adsorption / day desorption** cycles.

### Gaps to avoid competing on (for now)

- **Primary household water** — Watergen/Aquaria/Tsunami own high-volume grid-powered.
- **Desert primary supply at scale** — SOURCE and Atoco have years of field data.
- **Smart edge UI** — Resolved out of scope for passive path; Spout/Skywell own powered UX.
- **Industrial MOF efficiency** — AirJoule/Atoco have DARPA-grade R&D budgets.

### Recommended positioning statement (draft)

> **For** off-grid, preparedness, and cottage users in variable-humidity climates  
> **Who** need supplemental drinking water without grid power or roof infrastructure  
> **Unlike** refrigeration AWGs or fixed solar hydropanels  
> **passive-grid** is a portable, zero-power sorbent harvester with replaceable cartridges — honest emergency yield, expeditionary form factor, fraction of the cost.

## Threat assessment

### Threat matrix

| Threat | Severity | Timeline | Mitigation |
|--------|----------|----------|------------|
| **Refrigeration AWG price compression** (AquaFromAir, imports) | Low | Now | Different mechanism; passive-grid not competing on L/day |
| **SOURCE brand dominance in "water from air"** | Medium | Now | Differentiate on portability + price; avoid "hydropanel" framing |
| **Spout desiccant wheel in countertop** | Medium | 2026+ | Spout still grid-powered; passive-grid is grid-down |
| **AirJoule Core AWG residential launch** | High | Q4 2026 | MOF efficiency at scale; monitor pricing; stay in <$500 passive niche |
| **Atoco MOF commercialization** | High | 2026–2028 | Container/industrial first; patent MOF-303 locked — passive-grid uses open zeolite/SAPO-34 |
| **MOF research labs → spinouts** | Medium | Ongoing | CAU-10-H scaling ([New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/materials/cau-10-h-water-from-air-kiel-university/)); watch material cost curves |
| **DIY/open-source AWG community** | Low | Now | passive-grid CAD + print kit is differentiated productization |
| **Regulatory (Health Canada drinking water)** | Medium | Product launch | UV-C + filtration chain in powered variant; passive needs quality testing |

### Incumbent moats

- **SOURCE:** Installed base, 15-year service model, solar integration expertise, 10% RH marketing.
- **Watergen/Skywell:** Distribution, brand trust, high output in humid climates.
- **AirJoule/GE Vernova:** MOF IP, military contracts, waste-heat integration, DARPA validation.

### passive-grid defensibility (early)

- Open-material prototype path (zeolite/SAPO-34) avoiding MOF-303 patent lock
- CAD/print-kit + cartridge ecosystem (community + razor/blade)
- Expeditionary industrial design (MOLLE, parabolic reflector) — no clone exists
- First-mover in **consumer passive sorbent** if execution ships before AirJoule Core AWG downmarket move

# Deliverable — Phase 0 peer brief (CFO)

## In plain English
Finance checked whether a seasonal, event-based fresh lemonade stand can make money with a small self-funded setup. The story is per-cup and per-event profit after ingredients, booth fees, and labor—not investors or a software business. Under labeled mid-case assumptions, a typical festival day needs roughly sixty cups at about five dollars to break even; cheap local events need fewer. This finance peer brief is ready for the C-suite merge. Phase 0 is not closed, and you should not treat the mid-case numbers as a locked plan until you set price, place, and labor.

## What we found
- Intake classification stands (not rewritten): **Service** / explore / light / **bootstrapped**.
- Startup cash need (assumption): about **$535–$2,550** for cart/cooler/juicer and first supplies.
- Season-one all-in cash (assumption): about **$2.4k–$6.7k** across **8–12** event days — enough to prove or kill without fundraising.
- Mid-case cup math (assumptions): ~**$1.30** ingredient cost per cup, **$5** price → ~**$3.70** left per cup before booth/labor; mid-festival break-even ~**61 cups/day**.
- Fundraising (Phase **4B**) stays **skipped** unless you go multi-stand, hire every event, buy a trailer/commissary, or float large deposits.

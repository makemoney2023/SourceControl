## Summary

Lemonade Stand is a seasonal, event-based food service that sells ice-cold lemonade from freshly squeezed lemons. The opportunity is not “invent lemonade,” but to run a **repeatable event booth** with clear unit economics, permit-safe ops, and a positioning edge beyond category table stakes (fresh + cold). Phase 1 locks the problem/opportunity frame and labels assumptions so Phase 2–4 can pressure-test rather than invent a story.

## Problem statement

**For** event attendees who want a cold drink on hot, crowded days  
**Who** are underserved by warm fountain drinks, concentrate-heavy carts, or long food-truck lines  
**The** typical event lemonade / cold-drink option  
**Is a** commodity booth experience  
**That** often tastes flat, looks generic, and fails to justify a premium price  
**Unlike** a permanent café or packaged RTD brand  
**Our service** is a seasonal event stand that squeezes fresh lemons, serves ice-cold cups, and aims for a second differentiator (experience, speed, pack, or flavor system) so fresh+cold is necessary but not the whole story.

## Candidate solution directions

| Option | Description | Fit vs need | Feasibility now | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| **S1 — Classic event booth** | Single SKU (or small size ladder), fresh squeeze, ice, pop-up table/cart | High | High | Default explore path |
| **S2 — Experience booth** | Visible squeeze theater, tasting flight, or signature flavor system | High if premium price | Medium | Second differentiator candidate |
| **S3 — Organizer B2B kits** | Pre-contract with organizers for multi-event circuits | Medium | Low until geography known | Secondary; after S1 proof |
| **S4 — Packaged follow-on** | Bottled/RTD or merch brand extension | Low near-term | Low | Out of scope until event economics proven |

**Recommended framing:** Pursue **S1** as the core offer; design Phase 3 so **one** of S2’s edges (theater, flavor system, or speed/pack) is the working differentiator hypothesis. Defer S3/S4.
